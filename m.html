05 • SIGNAL

What is a Signal?

Signals are physical quantities that change with time.

Types of signals

  • Electrical signals
  • Radio waves
  • Light waves

Signals carry

  • Coded messages
  • Data
  • Information from sender to receiver
Signals are broadly divided into two groups: analogue signals and digital signals.
06 • ANALOGUE SIGNAL

Analogue Signal

An analogue signal is a continuous signal that changes continuously with time.

Representation

It is represented by a sine wave.

Continuous wave

Characteristics

It represents physical quantities that change continuously.

07 • ANALOGUE SIGNAL

Features and Examples

Characteristics

  • Analogue signals cannot be represented by 0 and 1.
  • They cannot be coded or compressed.
  • There is a greater chance of a decrease in data quality during transmission.

Examples

  • Sound waves
  • Light waves
  • Infrared radiations
  • Radio waves
  • Electricity
  • Hands of clocks
Analogue signals have a greater chance of data quality decreasing during transmission.
08 • DIGITAL SIGNAL

Digital Signal

A non-continuous electrical signal having discontinuous, discrete values with time is called a digital signal.

Representation

It is represented by square waves.

0 and 1

Characteristics

It represents physical quantities that change in segments.

It can be represented by 0 and 1.

09 • DIGITAL SIGNAL

Features and Examples

Characteristics

  • Digital signals can be coded and compressed.
  • There is less chance of data loss during transmission.
  • They can be stored in storage devices.

Examples

  • Mobile phones
  • Computers and monitors
  • Digital thermometers
  • Audio files
  • Video files
  • Digital photographs
Memory tip: Digital TV, Dish TV and satellite TV use digital signals.
10 • DIGITAL SIGNAL

Binary Combinations

Digital signals use two digits, 0 and 1. Different combinations can be formed using these digits.

Number of digits Possible combinations
1 0, 1
2 00, 01, 10, 11
3 000, 001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110, 111

Only two digits: 0 and 1

11 • COMPARISON

Analogue Signal vs Digital Signal

Analogue Signal Digital Signal
Continuous electrical signal that changes continuously with time. Non-continuous electrical signal having discrete values.
Represented by a sine wave. Represented by square waves.
Cannot be coded or compressed. Can be coded and compressed.
Greater chance of decrease in data quality during transmission. Less chance of data loss during transmission.

Sine wave = Continuous | Square wave = Discrete
